Tidied‑Up Version
Valerie, a retired teacher from a working‑class school, is brought on a sun holiday to Sicily by her daughter Hilary. Her husband, Kevin, stays at home in Ballynacargy in the Irish midlands, where ESB workers are expected to arrive to repair and upgrade local power lines. (Crucially, Ballynacargy is nowhere near the sea.)

Hilary organizes the entire trip. They rent a small flat close to the beach, and over the first few days they befriend the local lifeguard, the shopkeeper, and the ice‑cream seller.

A few days into the holiday, while they’re down by the beach, Valerie receives a phone call from an Italian man who says he is standing outside “the property.” Immediately, she’s suspicious: how did this stranger get her number? He insists it’s “on the records.” But Valerie didn’t book the accommodation — everything was arranged under Hilary’s name.

Growing agitated, and mindful of Sicily’s reputation for Mafia and organised crime, Valerie begins to interrogate him: “Who are you?” “How did you get my number?” Her distress is obvious enough that their new local friends notice something is wrong. When Hilary explains the situation, they agree to walk up together to confront this mysterious visitor and scare him off.

Valerie tells the man firmly, “We’ll be up in a few minutes — we’re just down at the beach.”

There’s a pause. Then the man replies, confused: “The beach?”

“Yes,” Valerie says. “Down in the Italian village.”

Another pause.

“But… I am in Ballynacargy.”

In that instant, everything clicks. He’s an ESB worker — the one expected at Valerie’s actual home in Ireland. Mortified, she realises she has just shouted at a perfectly innocent electrician who is standing outside her house, wondering why this irate woman in Sicily is demanding to know how he got her number.
